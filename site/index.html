Features

Built from notes you already keep.

A note is a meal or an ingredient because its tags say so. Folders play no part, so a vault with recipes in it needs no rearranging.

Log by ingredient, or by the meal you saved

The picker is organised by occasion. Tick ingredients one at a time, tick a saved meal, or build one out of parts and name it for next time. Every number describes one serving, and servings go up and down in halves.

  • Each occasion opens on what it has held lately, most recent first.
  • Copy yesterday's breakfast in one press while today's is still empty.
  • Correct one ingredient note and every meal built on it follows, on every day it was eaten.

A score that knows what time it is

Every bar is a floor, a ceiling or a range, and each pays out in proportion to how close it stands. The composite score is their weighted average. While the day's calories are still coming in, floors are measured against the share of the day you have eaten, so the number starts at 100 and moves with each meal.

  • Protein, fibre, potassium and calcium are floors. Sodium, fat and saturated fat are ceilings.
  • Carbs are drawn grey and never judged. Calories only count when over.
  • Tap the score for the three bars costing it most.

The week and the month, without blending them

"Am I on track right now" and "did the pattern hold" are different questions, so Day, Week and Month are different tabs. The week is a row a day with its meals and calories. The month is a calendar, a square a day, with the bars drawn as lines too thin to read and coloured so you do not have to.

  • Nuts, legumes and sweets are weekly groups in DASH, and are scored against the week.
  • A day you did not log is missing, not a zero.
  • Export any span as a markdown report, with a table that puts the largest sodium figure in bold.

It is all notes, and they stay yours

A meal is a markdown file with its numbers in frontmatter. A day's log is a note too, one per day, so a search, a backlink, a Dataview query and a backup all see what you ate. Edit the note and the day follows. Delete it and the day is cleared.

  • Works entirely offline. There is no telemetry, no account with the author and no server.
  • Sync however you already sync. A note arriving from another device is read the same way.
  • Open source under the MIT licence.

No. A note is a meal or an ingredient because its tags say so, and folders play no part. The folder setting only decides where Nosh files the notes it writes. Ingredients are grouped in the picker by the food group they contribute most to, worked out from the servings the note already carries.

Not unless you turn on Nosh AI and press a button that asks for it. Then what you typed into a draft, the note you are asking about, or the photo you took goes to the Anthropic API and nowhere else. There is no telemetry, no account with the author, and no file is read or written outside the vault.

Every bar has an editable target, a weight, and a shape: a floor to reach, a ceiling to stay under, a range, or a reference that is drawn but never judged. Type your calorie level and the DASH pattern scales to it. Then change any row, hide the ones you do not want, and the score, the colours and the prompts follow.

Nosh is not yet in the community plugin list. Install BRAT from the community list, choose Add a beta plugin, and give it brearleyjonathon/nosh. BRAT installs the latest release and keeps it updated. Or download three files from the latest release into your vault's plugins folder and enable Nosh under Community plugins. An empty vault can start from the sample notes in settings.
